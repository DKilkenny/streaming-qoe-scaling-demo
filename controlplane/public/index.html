What is this?

This is a small streaming backend with two decoupled paths: a read path that starts playback sessions and serves the catalog, and a write path that ingests QoE beacons (rebuffers, errors, completions) into a queue drained by a pool of workers.

Because the paths are decoupled, playback-start time (VST) stays fast even when the write pipeline is under heavy load — that separation is the point of the architecture, and what this demo is built to show.

The autoscaler watches utilization and backlog on the write path and adds or removes workers; a freshly provisioned worker takes ~12s to “warm up” before it helps.

Honesty note: VST, backlog, utilization, and worker counts are measured live from the running stack. The viewer traffic and each worker’s cold-start delay are simulated — they model the shape of a real premiere surge, not literal AWS provisioning times.

VST p95 (SLO <100ms) – Read-path latency, measured live. Decoupled from the write pipeline.
Concurrent streams – Live sessions being tracked right now.
Rebuffer ratio – Share of sessions that stalled. From client beacons.
Playback errors – Share of sessions that errored out. From client beacons.
Beacon backlog – Queued beacons waiting on workers.
Active workers – Processing the queue now. “+N warming” are cold-starting.
Utilization – Incoming beacon rate vs current worker capacity. Can exceed 100% while workers are still warming — that's the gap the scaler races to close.
